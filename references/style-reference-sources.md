# Category-Specific Style References

Use references to calibrate visual language, not to copy individual works or replace the user’s taste. Extract patterns such as framing, light, color, gesture, duration, pacing, camera movement, and story function. Likes, views, and platform ranking are not quality scores.

When a project lacks a style brief, consult a small, relevant set of examples and record the patterns used. Do not browse every source for every project.

## Landscape, travel, architecture, and human geography

- [500px](https://500px.com/) — polished landscape, travel, cityscape, and architectural still photography. Useful for composition, light, weather, depth, and viewpoint.
- [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/) — travel films, walking films, landscape cinematography, and longer-form pacing. Prefer established filmmakers and curated channels over search popularity alone.
- [ShotDeck](https://shotdeck.com/) — searchable film stills tagged by framing, lighting, color, composition, location, and emotion. Strong for cinematic shot comparison.
- [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/photography/) — landscape, culture, place, and human-context storytelling. Use to avoid reducing a destination to generic beauty shots.
- [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/) and [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/) — architecture, spatial sequence, material, scale, and the relationship between people and built environments.

## Portraits, family, friendship, and relationships

- [Documentary Family Awards](https://documentaryfamilyawards.com/) — direct reference for candid family dynamics, siblings, joy, everyday life, environmental portraits, and narrative family stories.
- [Family Photojournalist Association](https://www.fpja.com/) — natural family life, real settings, humor, affection, and unposed interaction.
- [LensCulture](https://www.lensculture.com/) — contemporary portrait, documentary, street, and visual-story series. Useful for sequencing and emotional ambiguity.
- [Magnum Photos](https://www.magnumphotos.com/) — documentary human relationships, decisive moments, context, and long-term visual storytelling.
- [This Is Reportage](https://thisisreportage.com/) — unposed relationship moments and story sequences; useful beyond weddings for gesture, anticipation, reaction, and group dynamics.
- [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/) — current portrait, family, lifestyle, and short-video expression. Follow specific photographers and filmmakers; do not use engagement as the selection standard.

## Video craft and emotional storytelling

- [Vimeo Staff Picks](https://vimeo.com/channels/staffpicks) — human-curated short films and documentaries. Useful for shot duration, visual transitions, sound-image relationships, and emotional payoff.
- [NOWNESS](https://www.nowness.com/) — art, culture, portrait, fashion, and authored short films. Useful for more expressive pacing and visual language.
- [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/) — family films, personal documentaries, travel Vlogs, and creator-specific editing grammar. Use named creators or curated playlists.

## Current Chinese and social-platform expression

- 小红书 — contemporary Chinese travel, portrait, family, lifestyle, and mobile-video conventions. Use it to understand current audience language, not as the sole quality benchmark.
- [X](https://x.com/) — discover photographers, filmmakers, behind-the-scenes threads, and reference links. Treat it as a discovery layer; compression, mixed context, and virality make it a weak primary benchmark.

## Calibration output

Before culling, summarize only the patterns relevant to the project, for example:

- preferred subject distance and framing;
- natural versus posed interaction;
- light and color character;
- what counts as an emotional peak;
- acceptable camera movement and shot duration;
- recurring filler patterns to deprioritize.
